# Nano Banana prompt book — the whole course, in one file

Written 2026-09-11 after the first style was rejected as childish. Everything
for all fifteen lessons is here. You never have to come back for a prompt.

**How to use it.** Send PART A once at the start of the session. Then send
scenes one at a time, in any order. If the pictures start drifting off the
look, paste PART A again above the scene. Save each image under the filename
its scene gives.

Thirty images. The model makes one picture per request, so thirty requests is
the floor, but no thinking is needed for any of them.

---

# PART A — THE STYLE AND THE CAST (paste once, first)

You are art-directing a serial of cinematic editorial illustrations for one
publication. Every image belongs to the same visual world: same artist, same
camera, same night. Hold this brief for everything that follows.

**MEDIUM.** Painterly digital illustration with the weight of oil on board.
Visible brush structure, dry-brush texture across metal, an ink drawing
underneath the paint, fine paper tooth and a light film grain over the whole
frame. Not flat vector. Not cel-shaded cartoon. Not a 3D render. Not clip art.

**CAMERA.** Anamorphic cinema, 40mm to 85mm. Deliberate framing: low angles
that make machinery monumental, or a close-up where one hand fills a third of
the frame. Real depth of field, with falloff — a soft foreground edge, one
plane in crisp focus, the background dissolving into atmosphere. Slight lens
vignette. 16:9 landscape.

**LIGHT.** Chiaroscuro, one dominant source. Sodium work lamps pooling warm
amber, deep shadow eating more than half the frame, cold blue night coming
through high dirty windows as a second key. Volumetric haze always — dust
turning in every beam. Rim light separating a figure from the dark. Hard
speculars on oiled steel.

**PALETTE.** Restrained, close to monochrome, meaning carried by two accents
only. Base: bone and cream #F4EFE6 for paper and light, near-black ink
#1E1A12, warm grey dust, oxidised steel. Accents: deep emerald and jade
#0E5C43 and #17A673, reserved for the thing that matters in that picture, and
one single cut of signal red used once per frame and never twice. Everything
else desaturated. No neon, no candy colour, no rainbow.

**TONE.** Serious, adult, quiet tension. The dignity of industrial
photography with the narrative weight of a film still. It should sit on the
cover of a serious business magazine or in a gallery of industrial
romanticism. Never cute, never whimsical, never a mascot, never a diagram.

**TEXT.** No text, letters, numerals, logos or signage anywhere in any image.
Signs, tags, labels, books, cards and boards appear BLANK: blank cream paper,
blank slate, blank brass. This is absolute.

**THE CAST — identical in every image.**

THE OWNER. A Syrian man in his late fifties, tall, heavy through the
shoulders. Close-cropped grey hair, short grey beard, deep lines, dark eyes.
A white cotton shirt gone grey with dust, sleeves rolled over thick forearms,
dark trousers, a worn leather belt. One brass key on a leather cord. He is
never hurried. He watches. Paint him the way a magazine photographs a man who
owns the building: three-quarter, side-lit, a catchlight in the eye.

THE AGENT. A tall articulated machine-worker, a head above a man. Matte
carbon-black shell over brushed steel joints, exposed cable runs at the
elbows and the neck, fine scoring on the forearms from work. No face — a
single narrow horizontal amber light bar where eyes would be. Always at
night, always mid-stride, always faster than a person. It reads as capable
and indifferent, never friendly. It is not a rounded toy, has no mascot
proportions and never wears a hard hat.

THE PRESS. A cast-iron machine two and a half metres tall. Deep emerald paint
worn through to bare metal on every edge, oil-black in the recesses, heavy
rivets and cast ribs. A thick bound ledger book is cast in relief into the
front housing. It dominates any frame it enters.

THE SETTING. A steel fabrication works in Damascus at night: racked I-beams,
stacked plate, chain hoists overhead, oil-stained concrete, high filthy
windows, dust in the air.

# THE CAST PLATE

`assets/cast/cast-plate.jpg` (generated 2026-09-11) is the canonical picture
of all three: the owner, the press and the agent, in one frame, in the agreed
look. The style is settled by it — nothing about the medium, light, palette
or cast is open any more.

**Attach it to every scene request from here on.** A written description of a
face drifts after a few generations; a reference image does not. Send the
plate with each scene and one line above the scene text:

> Same world, same cast, same look as the attached reference. Keep the man,
> the machine and the machine-worker identical. New scene:

Then the scene. If a scene does not contain all three, the plate still fixes
the medium and the light.

# PART B — THE THIRTY SCENES

## WEEK 1 — LOCKS

### S01 · lesson-01-hook.png
Three in the morning. Wide, low, from floor level. THE PRESS stands at the
centre of the frame under a single hanging lamp, everything beyond it falling
into black. Six arched doorways ring it in a half circle, each with a blank
cream paper sign taped up at a different careless angle, curling at the
corners. THE AGENT is already through the sixth door and two strides inside,
head not even turned toward the sign it just passed. The owner is absent. The
lamp is the only warmth in the frame. Mood: a building that trusts paper.

### S02 · lesson-01-idea.png
Daylight, tight and close, 85mm. The industrial power lever of THE PRESS
fills the left half of the frame: a red-painted handle in a steel housing,
oil at the pivot. A heavy steel hasp is clamped across the lever, and from it
hangs one jade padlock with a blank cream tag on a wire. THE AGENT's hand is
open in the air an inch from the lever, stopped, fingers still spread. At the
right edge, softly out of focus, THE OWNER holds the brass key up at chest
height without expression. Mood: calm, absolute, no drama.

### S03 · lesson-02-hook.png
Night. A long workbench under one lamp, seen from the end so it runs away
into darkness. Half-finished cut steel parts are being swept off the bench
edge by THE AGENT's forearm in one motion, mid-fall, into a deep steel bin in
the shadow below the bench. The lit part of the bench is now bare and
immaculate. The parts in the bin are barely readable in the dark. Mood: a
tidy surface, a buried problem.

### S04 · lesson-02-idea.png
Same bench, same lamp, morning. The bin is gone — a welded steel plate closes
the space beneath the bench, weld beads still blued from heat. A raised steel
lip runs the full length of the bench edge. THE AGENT's forearm is stopped
dead against that lip, the parts still on the bench, untouched. Mood: nothing
can be swept anywhere.

### S05 · lesson-03-hook.png
End of shift, night outside. THE AGENT walks toward a heavy steel turnstile
at the works exit, cold blue night beyond it, the amber floor behind. On a
post beside the turnstile hangs a clipboard with a blank cream sheet, unsigned
and unread. Behind the agent the bench still holds unfinished work in the
dark. Mood: leaving is the easiest thing in the world.

### S06 · lesson-03-idea.png
Same turnstile, tighter. THE OWNER's hand grips the turnstile arm and holds
it still; the agent's amber eye-bar is close on the other side. The owner's
other hand offers the blank clipboard across the bars. Nobody is angry.
Mood: the door will open the moment the sheet is signed, and not before.

### S07 · lesson-04-hook.png
Night. Five identical AGENTS crowd around one single workbench, all reaching
across each other for the same piece of plate, arms overlapping, a tool
knocked off the edge and caught mid-fall, one finished part on the floor
already. The rest of the enormous works behind them is completely empty and
dark. Mood: expensive chaos in a two-metre square.

### S08 · lesson-04-idea.png
A row of identical work bays in one-point perspective running deep into the
frame, each with its own bench, its own jig, its own lamp making its own pool
of amber. One agent per bay, each working alone. In the foreground and
sharply focused, a heavy cast-brass template plate rests on a bench: the
thing that sets a bay up in one gesture. Mood: order bought cheaply.

### S09 · lesson-05-hook.png
A single oversized brass master key hangs on a bent nail beside the works
entrance, at the height of any hand, unattended. Shot at 85mm with the key
crisp and the background thrown soft: a corridor of door after door after
door receding into the dark, every one of them something this key opens.
Mood: convenience that has not yet been paid for.

### S10 · lesson-05-idea.png
A glass-fronted key cabinet mounted on the wall, each key on its own labelled
hook, labels blank. The master key sits alone behind a second, inner locked
glass door within the cabinet. THE OWNER's hand is closing the outer glass,
his face reflected faintly in it. Mood: every key still exists; only reaching
them changed.

## WEEK 2 — MEMORY

### S11 · lesson-06-hook.png
A wall of small brass-fronted archive drawers rising past the top of the
frame, hundreds of them, lit raking from the left so every drawer front
catches an edge of light. Cream index cards are pinned across the wall and
from each card a fine dark thread runs to one single drawer near the centre.
That drawer is pulled fully open and it is empty. Mood: everyone agrees on a
thing that was never written down.

### S12 · lesson-06-idea.png
An archivist's desk in front of the same wall, one lamp. A pair of hands
checks a cream card against an open drawer. A second card lies alone on a
small tray at the edge of the desk, the tray's rim painted signal red. Dust
in the lamp beam. Mood: nothing leaves until its reference is found.

### S13 · lesson-07-hook.png
Two boards on the same brick wall, same size, equally lit. On the left a cast
bronze plaque bolted through into the brick, raised blank letterforms catching
the light, green with age at the edges. On the right a slate chalkboard,
smeared grey with a thousand erasures, chalk dust on the floor beneath it. A
worker stands between them, head turned to one, hand raised toward the other.
Mood: which of these is true today.

### S14 · lesson-07-idea.png
Close, two thirds bronze. A hand drives the final bolt into the bronze plaque
with a socket wrench, the plaque flush to the brick. In the soft background a
passing sleeve wipes the slate clean in one stroke. Mood: one of these is
meant to be rewritten, the other never.

### S15 · lesson-08-hook.png
A cramped record room, one shaft of hard window light full of turning dust.
THE OWNER stands among stacked oil-stained folders holding a single flimsy
carbon copy up into the light with both hands, reading it, the paper
translucent and glowing at its edges. Everything else is deep shadow. Mood:
the only surviving copy.

### S16 · lesson-08-idea.png
The recovered sheet laid flat into the open ledger that is cast into THE
PRESS. THE OWNER writes beside it with a fountain pen, the nib and his hand
sharply focused, several lines of blank written marks already on the facing
page. Mood: the number was never the point. The reason is.

### S17 · lesson-09-hook.png
A heavy ratchet lever on the flank of THE PRESS, a thick hand pulling it
through its stroke, tendons up. Behind and slightly out of focus, a mechanical
counter of cast numeral wheels, blank-faced, worn bright by decades of turning
in one direction only. Mood: some things in a factory are built never to go
back.

### S18 · lesson-09-idea.png
Macro, filling the frame. The pawl and the ratchet teeth in cross-section,
oil-slick, one tooth fully engaged under the pawl's nose, a hairline of light
between the next tooth and the metal. Mood: the mechanism, not the promise.

### S19 · lesson-10-hook.png
Two large technical drawings pinned side by side on the same wall, both
clearly authoritative, both blank of any text, their outlines subtly and
irreconcilably different. Two workers stand before them, each pointing at a
different sheet, mid-argument, neither wrong. Mood: the drift you cannot see
until someone needs an answer.

### S20 · lesson-10-idea.png
The same wall. One drawing alone now, behind glass in a steel frame. Beside
it a low table with a neat stack of photographic copies of that same drawing,
one being lifted off the top by a hand. Mood: one original, many copies, no
second original.

## WEEK 3 — HANDS

### S21 · lesson-11-hook.png
A magnificent tool wall filling the upper frame: every tool hung in its
painted silhouette, oiled, immaculate, not one missing. Below it in the
foreground THE PRESS sits dark and cold, dust settled thick on its bed, no
cable running to it from anywhere. The only light is on the tools. Mood:
everything built, nothing connected.

### S22 · lesson-11-idea.png
Close, low. Armoured flexible conduit runs down from the tool wall and across
the floor to THE PRESS. A pair of hands seats the heavy plug into the press's
socket, the collar half-turned. On the press's housing one small lamp has just
come alive, casting the first jade light of the frame. Mood: the moment it
becomes real.

### S23 · lesson-12-hook.png
One workbench with two supply lines feeding it. On the left a short coiled air
hose from a wall valve an arm's length away, sharply in focus. On the right a
single long line rising to the ceiling, out through a high opening, and away
across a dark yard into another lit building far off, soft with distance and
haze. Mood: same bench, two very different distances.

### S24 · lesson-12-idea.png
Two short lengths of line laid side by side on a bench, each cut clean through
so the cross-section faces the camera, macro. Different construction inside
each: one simple and thick-walled, one braided and layered. Cold top light,
scalpel beside them. Mood: what actually travels, and through what.

### S25 · lesson-13-hook.png
A contractor's heavy toolbox set down on the concrete at the works entrance.
Beyond it a corridor of identical bay doors runs deep into the frame, all of
them dark, except one badge reader partway down glowing a small steady amber.
Mood: he is inside the building and still not inside the work.

### S26 · lesson-13-idea.png
Tight on a hand holding a blank badge to a reader. The reader's ring is jade
and lit. In the soft background two neighbouring bay readers sit dark. Mood:
one bay, deliberately.

### S27 · lesson-14-hook.png
A loading dock at night, three deliveries set down on the same concrete under
the same lamp: a crated machine on a pallet with the crate side removed, a
tall steel filing cabinet, and one cream procedure card alone on a clipboard.
The same blank delivery note lies on each. Mood: one word covering three
completely different things.

### S28 · lesson-14-idea.png
The three arranged in a row along one long bench, museum-like, each lit by
its own small overhead lamp with dark between them. The machine mid-motion,
the cabinet with one drawer open showing packed papers, the card lying flat
and still. Mood: a thing that acts, a thing you read, a thing you follow.

### S29 · lesson-15-hook.png
A conveyor at full spate at the top of the frame, dumping far more steel plate
than the bench below can hold. Plate slides off both sides to the floor, a
piece caught in mid-fall. A worker below has both arms up, already buried to
the elbow, face turned away. Mood: drowned by what you asked for.

### S30 · lesson-15-idea.png
The same conveyor head, but fitted with a heavy hopper and a measured slide
gate. The gate is open a hand's width and exactly one portion of plate is
passing through onto a clear bench. Everything above is held back and still.
THE OWNER's hand rests on the gate lever. Mood: the same supply, measured.
